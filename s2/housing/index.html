Affordable Housing

Points for neighborhood discussion

Our Cheney created these discussion points to prompt a conversation about potential options. They are not endorsed by any organization and were not developed with Atlanta Track Club, Atlanta Public Schools, or the City of Atlanta. The only purpose is a discussion point to gather community feedback about potential paths forward.

The images are generated from Blender and use U.S. Geological Survey terrain data, USDA aerial photographs, and community-maintained OpenStreetMap streets. The models use real-world dimensions and elevations to illustrate how the proposed buildings and uses could fit on the site.

A stated goal of both the City and the APS Board is affordable housing. This discussion point is to add housing at Cheney Field and the Martin Street/McGill lot, while retaining the rest of the site as greenspace and parkland.

Cheney Field

Cheney Field has enough room for 9–10 townhomes, with access from either Georgia Avenue or Connally Street. The remainder of Cheney Field would be retained as greenspace and used as soccer fields.

Martin Street/McGill lot

The Martin Street/McGill lot already has defined lots that could be developed with single-family homes. Access would be from Ami St and the remainder would be retained as greenspace.

Affordable housing on APS land

Former Atlanta Public Schools properties are helping create affordable housing across Atlanta. Completed developments and projects now moving forward offer examples of how school-owned land can serve neighborhood housing needs.

Academy Lofts at Adair Park

Completed

The former Adair Elementary School, acquired from APS, was converted into 35 apartments, including affordable housing, plus commercial and community space.

Sources: Developer’s overview · Invest Atlanta

The Beacon at Cooper Street

Completed; opened April 2026

This permanent supportive housing development in Mechanicsville was built on former APS land transferred to the City through a land swap. The exchange also provided land for Hope-Hill Elementary School.

Sources: Land-swap announcement · Opening announcement

Lakewood Heights Elementary

Approved; predevelopment

APS and Atlanta Urban Development selected a developer to preserve the historic school and create mixed-income housing and commercial space. The Atlanta Board of Education approved the development agreement and ground lease in September 2026.

Source: Lakewood Heights project update

Peeples Street Elementary

Planned

Plans for the former school site in West End call for approximately 70 rental and homeownership units. Announced affordability targets cover 30% of homes: 20% for households earning up to 50% of area median income and another 10% for households earning up to 80%.

Source: Peeples Street redevelopment announcement
