A Compromise Proposal From Our Cheney

One building.
Two seasons.

A full indoor competition track.
A public recreation center.
The same building, throughout the year.

See how the seasons work ↓

The central idea

A fieldhouse for
the whole year.

We believe that it is possible for a single building to serve both competitive athletics and community recreation throughout the year. This compromise proposal from Our Cheney provides a viable path forward to meet the community and Atlanta Track Club's needs.

SHAC would provide a full indoor competition track without dedicating a large public building to one sport year-round.

From December through March, it would operate primarily as an indoor track-and-field competition venue. From April through November, movable athletic surfaces and retractable seating would turn the same floor into a community recreation and tournament facility.

01 / December through March

Competition season.

Keep what track needs.
Keep the building simple.

The concept retains the essentials: a full 200-meter competition oval, interior sprint lanes, field-event areas, adequate clear span and ceiling height, and spectator seating.

The working concept contains roughly 1,000–1,500 spectator seats, with substantial seating movable or retractable so it does not dictate the layout for the other eight months.

Same shell. Same floor.↓A different configuration.

02 / April through November

Recreation season.

Open the floor.
Let the community play.

Retract the competition seating, cover and protect competition-only surfaces, and install movable sports floors. The same fieldhouse becomes a public recreation center, with space for everyday play and tournaments.

The drawings illustrate three basketball courts, tennis and pickleball, with volleyball another possible court configuration. Indoor running and walking continue around the larger recreation floor.

Established ideas, combined

The building stays.
The surface changes.

The permanent asset is a large, flat floor inside a clear-span building. Athletic finishes can change with the season.

Portable courts already exist. SHAC would bring those systems together with a protected competition floor and retractable seating. The diagrams below explain the idea; the actual floor assembly and compatible surfaces would need design work.

Designed to change seasons

An arena conversion.
Twice a year.

This is not rebuilding the building twice a year. Like an arena changeover, it is a planned operation that swaps equipment and playing surfaces. It likely can be accomplished in a day and no more than few.

  1. 01

    Clear & store

    Remove competition equipment and retract or move seating into its storage position.

  2. 02

    Protect & install

    Protect competition-only areas, then roll out or assemble recreation surfaces and set up equipment.

  3. 03

    Reverse for winter

    Store recreation floors and equipment, uncover competition surfaces, and restore the track configuration.

Design for the changeover from day one. Adjacent storage and easy floor access should make the spring and winter conversions routine operations.

Parking & arrival

Park underneath.
Leave room for the park.

The concept places parking underneath the building, taking advantage of the site’s natural change in elevation.

Keeping parking within the building footprint avoids consuming additional land with a large surface lot. Pairing that approach with shuttles and drop-offs for major events would allow substantially more surrounding land to remain park and green space.

Everyday recreation

Parking for everyday users.

Below-building parking would support ordinary recreation-center visits, within the site’s limited capacity.

Track competitions

Spectators: drop-off and shuttle-drop-off entry only.

Visitors arrive by shuttle, rideshare, team bus, or drop-off. With a single controlled entrance access can be limited to shuttle and drop off only. Visitors that attempt to park in the neighborhood will not be allowed access. Limited on-site parking remains for uses that actually require it, including accessible parking and essential operations.

Where the compromise belongs

Why build it
this way?

A 200-meter track cannot meaningfully be made half-size.

Give track competition the space it needs, and make the compromises elsewhere:

  • Simplify the building. A straightforward clear-span fieldhouse.
  • Reduce permanent spectator infrastructure. Seating that can move out of the way.
  • Avoid massive surface parking. Park underneath and plan for event shuttles.
  • Make the interior convertible. Different surfaces and equipment for different seasons.
  • Get more public use from the same footprint. Four months of track competition and eight months of recreation.

This compromise would give track competition a legitimate home for four months without making Cheney a single-purpose track complex for twelve. Clear-span fieldhouses, retractable seating, removable courts, portable hardwood and turf, and seasonal arena conversions already exist. The proposal is to investigate how those ideas could work together here.

The long-term public asset

Built for a season.
Useful for generations.

Think of this as a fieldhouse that happens to be configured around indoor track first.

If Atlanta Track Club or another competition operator eventually moved to a larger purpose-built venue, the shell, slab, utilities, parking, and recreation infrastructure would remain useful.

The building could become a full-time public basketball, volleyball, pickleball, tennis, and indoor-sports center. The replaceable sports surfaces are not the long-lived asset. The building is.

An idea worth investigating

Our Cheney does not ask whether it should be a track facility or a community recreation facility.

It asks whether the same building can successfully be both.
