Four principles for a fair agreement

The Ask

Summerhill should welcome major investment from a position of strength. Before public land is committed for generations, the neighborhood deserves a clear agreement that protects local control, limits harm, shares value fairly, and keeps the building sustainable over its full life.

Four asks

Neighborhood Controlled Parks

Local Control

The neighborhood must take a permanent controller role in the future of these public lands. Ownership, stewardship, and long-term decision-making should be structured so that Summerhill residents are not merely consulted, but decide how the land is used, protected, and maintained.

A major facility will affect traffic, parking, construction, noise, public access, safety, and daily life. Those impacts should be identified in advance and addressed through a clear, funded plan developed with the people who will live beside the project.

Ensure the project works for everyone

Neighborhood Protections

A fair economic return

Shared Value

The land being contributed is worth tens of millions of dollars. In return, the neighborhood should receive legally binding economic commitments of comparable significance—not informal promises or benefits that can disappear when leadership changes.

The final agreement should create lasting value for Summerhill and clearly explain how the public shares in the opportunity created on public land.

The building should have a credible plan for maintenance, major repairs, capital replacement, and eventual demolition or site restoration.

Summerhill and the public should not inherit a deteriorated facility, unfunded liabilities, or land that can no longer be used when the building reaches the end of its life.

Protect the public from future costs

Sustainability

Fairness makes success durable

A great project needs a great agreement.

These asks are not barriers to the indoor track. They are the foundation of a fair partnership—one that respects Summerhill’s land, protects its residents, and leaves the neighborhood stronger for generations.
